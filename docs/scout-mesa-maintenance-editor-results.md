# Mesa maintenance and editor pilot

**Twelve resources reviewed; all twelve retained; three text updates.** This was a small recheck of the earlier Mesa editorial draft, not another whole-office run. It produced useful changes without requiring a new playbook lesson.

## Questions and decisions

**All fourteen existing questions, IDs and histories were preserved.** Two new questions ask whether TSO can use PINCH and which Worker housing arrangement/follow-up applies. None was marked human-resolved.

The deposit resource already contains two overlapping eligibility questions. One call can cover both; the review identifies that overlap without deleting either question's provenance. The Housing Authority and deposit resource also share contact details, but provide distinct voucher and deposit routes. They remain separate resources.

Several apparent omissions turned out to be deliberate, useful cautions:

- **Mesa deposits:** the public page says 80% of area median income; the May 2025 manual says 60% and requires current Mesa residence. Both remain accessible. I initially suggested restoring the public-page number, then withdrew that suggestion after reading the saved question's evidence. The draft was never changed to that number. [Public page](https://www.mesaaz.gov/Resident-Resources/Housing/Human-Services/DepositAssistanceProgram), [Manual, page 4](https://www.mesaaz.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/government/grants/communitydev/final-com-secdep-tbra-policy-manual-5.13.25.pdf)
- **Family Housing Hub:** its site emphasizes staying reachable; the 211 listing still calls for weekly check-in without giving the check-in number. The existing question is justified. [Hub](https://www.fhhub.org/), [211 listing](https://search.211arizona.org/search/186386a4-2715-5d3e-aa4a-78735d01a64a)
- **Smart Justice:** the same county page asks for photo ID in one place and a driver's license in another. Missing a driver's license should not become an invented denial of services. [County instructions](https://www.maricopa.gov/3783/Smart-Justice)

Other retained resources were Helen's Hope Chest, Friends at First, Dress for Success, Express Employment, Goodwill and House of Refuge. Their specialized audiences or unresolved details did not justify excluding them. Dress for Success's services page remained inaccessible, although its education form corroborated the saved main contact. That is incomplete retrieval, not closure evidence.

## What the extra pass contributed

The primary review was frozen before follow-up. The targeted pass made eight additional document requests addressing specific uncertainties. It corroborated and reconciled **four useful finding units across two resources**: Worker requirements, conflicting housing descriptions, its published wait, and PINCH access. The leads were already visible in primary search results; these are not four discoveries by an independent researcher.

It also reconfirmed existing deposit and Hub conflicts. The alternate Dress for Success URL failed; its education form did not settle funding eligibility. Those attempts are recorded separately from new useful findings.

The initial research phase took about **three minutes** of coordinator wall time; research and draft preparation together took about **seven minutes**, excluding later reporting and verification. Exact phase timestamps and tool measurements are saved. The cycle made 32 explicit page/document requests, six search queries and one reread of a document excerpt. Search results exposed additional pages; PDF page totals differ from document counts. The intended two-page allocation per resource was exceeded for some records while staying within the total request allowance. This was a practical pilot, not standardized research-effort measurement.

No separate model-service duration, dollar cost or human curation time was measured. The useful finding is that a **targeted follow-up can clarify consequential details and confirm why a question remains**, without repeating every resource. One batch does not establish an optimal stopping rule or model schedule.

## Learning and the next increment

Scout imported twelve attributable editorial decisions and five package-change observations into its learning queue. These are **seventeen evidence records, not seventeen independent learning examples**. They describe three edited resources, including two new questions. Existing verification fields were unchanged; no subsequent human-confirmed package was available among the saved Mesa packages checked.

No single proposed method had the predeclared three independently supported mistakes. No lesson was proposed, tested or activated. The repeated conflicts instead supply counterexamples against blindly filling in or deleting curator questions. My deposit oversight is one recorded near-miss, not proof that Scout repeatedly makes that mistake.

This cycle exercised Scout's package and question handling, common resource app, editorial evidence import, package comparison and feedback queue. **It did not run the multi-provider maintenance scheduler or an independent editor/model comparison.** Research and editing were performed by the current coordinator in the same context.

**Increment 5 remains the next numbered increment**, with adaptive passes and model sampling still pending evidence. The useful next iteration is another bounded batch or, preferably when available, a package carrying actual curator answers. We should retain the measured evidence without requiring each batch to produce a lesson.
