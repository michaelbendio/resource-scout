# Populating a new office with good resources: six ideas — 30 September 2026

From Claude, at Michael's request, after the list-seeding trial
([`list-seeding-trial-claude-20260930.md`](list-seeding-trial-claude-20260930.md) on branch
`claude/list-seeding-trial-20260930`). The aim throughout: about 10 to 30 resources a
missionary would hand out, per category, found cheaply enough that a successor can run
Scout within a budget.

## 1. A checklist of the kinds of resource every office needs

Provo's 191 human-curated resources are the model of what good looks like, and nearly every
office needs the same *kinds* in each category: the county housing authority, the homeless
front door (coordinated entry), the main food bank, the domestic-violence shelter, WIC, the
SNAP office, 211, legal aid and so on. List those kinds once, 5 to 8 per category. For a new
office Scout then finds the local instance of each kind rather than researching
open-endedly, and stops when every kind is found. This is "search by need" made concrete,
and it gives Scout a natural stopping point.

## 2. Research state-wide and national resources once, and share them

Welfare Square, Provo, Ogden, Logan and Brigham City share Utah's resources (DWS, Medicaid,
211 Utah, Utah Legal Services, the state crisis line), and 988, Lifeline and the national
domestic-violence hotline are the same everywhere. Research that layer once per state, and
once nationally, then reuse it. Each office's run covers only its local layer: probably a
third to a half of the work saved for every office after the first in a state.

## 3. Ask the Church's own people first

Every TSO office probably has a sheet or binder, as Provo had `provo.html`. Bishops'
storehouses, Deseret Industries, Family Services and the self-reliance employment centres
are ready-made entries, and stake welfare specialists often keep local lists. These are the
most reliable leads there are and cost nothing to get; Scout's job becomes checking and
completing them.

## 4. Launch with the starter sets only

A new office could go live with about 10 curated resources a category, around 200 in all,
which is Provo's size and enough to be useful. The reserve can wait until use shows where
the gaps are. It is a smaller first job for the office's curators, and missionaries see
only reviewed resources.

## 5. Let use guide what comes next

The printed-resources report (WSRS-TSO, `docs/print-report-design.md`) now shows what
missionaries actually hand out. Unreviewed resources that get printed are the first to
curate; curated ones never printed in six months are candidates for review or removal; and
the same counts can order re-verification by phone, so the most-printed agencies are called
first.

## 6. Re-check the lists cheaply

Once an office is seeded from its local lists, re-reading those same few pages every month
or two catches most closures and new programmes: a few page reads, not a Scout run. That is
the upkeep a successor could afford.

## Together

Ideas 1 and 2 change Scout from open-ended research into "fill in the checklist, locally",
cheaper and easier to hand over. The first test, drawing the checklist from Provo and
checking it against Welfare Square's starter sets, is in
`kinds-checklist-trial-20260930.md` on the same branch.
