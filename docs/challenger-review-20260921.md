# Requested Codex review: DeepSeek and Grok challenger findings

Michael requested “Review them then and give me your thoughts.” This completes the
requested review of the **isolated three-category comparison**, including its
finding aids and proposed human review order. Production remains paused. No new
DeepSeek, Grok, Claude, research, curation, or paid review worker was launched.

My judgment: **DeepSeek earned a place in Scout's discovery workflow.** It produced
more useful distinct options in this sample at a very low inference cost. The
results do not support discarding Grok's saved work or assuming one model finds
every important service. For Michael's current budget, I would prefer another
bounded DeepSeek evaluation, followed by Codex curation and a deliberate check for
missed high-consequence services, before spending more on Grok. This is a
recommendation, not an authorized production architecture change or restart.

There are 113 resources in the union, 14 shared. Start here contains 48 unique
resources: eight shared, 18 DeepSeek-only, 22 Grok-only. After removing already

DeepSeek-only and 15 Grok-only. This is **category-relative** novelty against the
three sealed primary packets, not a search of all 21 Categories' office inventory.

The priority judgments were made resource by resource, with reasons, source-text
evidence, and access questions; no fixed quotas or automated scoring were used.
The reviewer knows the experiment and earlier results, so this is not a blinded,
independent statistical evaluation. Priority means **what to vet first**, not
provider quality, guaranteed availability, or human approval. Every retained
resource remains available regardless of its tier.

DeepSeek's larger set includes substantial service variety, not merely extra
staffing companies or duplicate names. Its distinct Start here contributions
include foster-youth vouchers, Hire Heroes, Upwardly Global, accessible books,
captioned telephone service, Medicare counseling, donated dental care, and the
long-term-care ombudsman. Grok's distinct contributions include REACH, home repair,
weatherization, UCAT, Medicaid/community-care pathways, infant sensory support,
and caregiver compensation. Examples are discovery attribution within this trial;
both providers' submissions were subsequently corrected by the same Codex curators.

Grok has a denser first-priority set overall (30/50 versus 26/77), but eight of its
first-priority resources were already covered by the primary, compared with one
of DeepSeek's. That distinction is why the marginal first-priority comparison is
25 versus 22. DeepSeek also has more specialized/additional alternatives: 38/13
versus Grok's 18/2. These are legitimate options, but each adds human vetting work.

DeepSeek research inference was about **$0.2575** in the completed trial. This
excludes web relay, Codex curation, supervision, and this review; it is not the
cost of producing 77 office-approved resources. The common curation took 39.27
worker minutes before this review. Saved Grok usage telemetry was $2.9928, not a
new charge or a fully controlled end-to-end cost comparison. Grok 4.7 was not tested.

## Corrections and consequential limitations

All 113 accepted identities remain. Eight Information records were corrected or
clarified, and one overbroad secondary Category membership was removed, leaving
124 Category memberships. Native results and earlier reports/drafts are unchanged.
Before/after text, sources, category-result hashes, and dispositions are preserved
in the [review audit](challenger-review-20260921.json).

- **FYI voucher eligibility:** the curator retained a 90-day foster-care departure
  window from older guidance. Current [HUD FYI guidance](https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/public-indian-housing-fyi)
  says 180 days and identifies Notice PIH 2026-19. Corrected the text; local referral
  acceptance, available vouchers, and lease-up support still require confirmation.
- **GEO federal reentry:** the court page says 95 beds while the
  [2026 GEO filing](https://investors.geogroup.com/node/18706/html) lists 115.
  Recorded the conflict and removed the implication that either number establishes
  current placement availability. This remains a federal placement, not an open shelter.
- **Public Guardian:** expanded last-resort criteria and the documentation/assessment
  requirement from the [referral guide currently linked by OPG](https://daas.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Opg-referral-information-2022.docx-1.pdf).
  That guide is dated 2022 and says age 18-plus; an old historical rule suggesting
  21 was a false alarm, not a correction to impose. Current screening needs confirmation.
- **WWP career services:** made the post-9/11 population and registration gate
  visible using the [program FAQ](https://www.woundedwarriorproject.org/programs/warriors-to-work).
  Military affiliation alone does not establish eligibility.
- **Navigation-relevant details:** added HEAT's stated household priorities;
  reconciled USDB's hearing/vision Parent Infant pathways; made Canine Companions'
  adult hearing-dog route explicit; and exposed EHDI parent-to-parent support in
  Information. These clarify pathways already present in the submissions or resource
  descriptions. Sources are preserved with each revision.
- **Category fit:** CPSS is an Employment training/certification resource for people
  with lived recovery experience. Removed its Disability cross-listing; it does
  not itself deliver disability care or disability-specific work accommodation.

The omission audit also matters. DeepSeek's Splore submission incorrectly described
it as separate from the National Ability Center. [NAC's history](https://nationalabilitycenter.org/history/)
confirms it joined NAC in 2017, and NAC still offers adaptive rafting. The sealed
primary already includes NAC and rafting, so retain the alias information without
creating another distinct service count; a failed old-site fetch is not closure.
Grok's [Work Success page](https://jobs.utah.gov/jobseeker/worksuccess.html) currently
says “Coming soon,” supporting the decision to leave current stand-alone intake
unconfirmed. Original decisions are preserved alongside these review amendments.

Other omitted leads—child-care subsidies, legal record clearance, document-fee
waivers, pet boarding, and referral directories—can remove real access barriers.
Their exclusion from this **direct-service category comparison** does not mean they
are worthless or should disappear from an appropriately categorized office inventory.
The All Means All directory remains preserved separately as navigation.

This was a full desk review of the saved corpus, dispositions, identities, merges,
category fit, and access uncertainty, with targeted live primary-source checks.
It was not a fresh crawl of every cited link, a telephone audit, or a systematic
provider error-rate study. Errors corrected in common curation/review cannot be
attributed as model accuracy rates. No Maryland/Washington-County geography mix-up
was identified; national and out-of-county services were retained where access
was supported, with travel and local capacity limitations explicit.
