# Codex content review — Housing checklist trial

All 16 provisional records were assessed against the public evidence gathered on
30 September 2026, the five-section preparation contract and all four office-fit
rules. This is an evaluation review, not a production completion event or human
approval. No legacy workbench or editor was generated, so browser/editor readiness
is not claimed. The final JSON fingerprint binds this review in `review`.

## Decisions that matter

- **Direct routes:** retained records have a public application, program phone or
  direct contact route. Milestone's form is named a referral application, but the
  county invites applicants to apply and provides the manager's direct phone.
  Its form could not be parsed; no claim is made that it was submitted or fully
  tested. SSVF's public outreach/appointment route is used, not its residents-only
  outreach sessions. Homebuyer counseling is usable without a lender referral.
- **Shelters:** no ordinary overnight human shelter was carried into Housing.
  HomeInn, Milestone and LifeStart offer actual residential housing programs.
  Ruff Haven remains because pet crisis sheltering is an explicit Housing kind in
  this trial; its record clearly says that it houses pets, not people.
- **Geography:** the trial area is Salt Lake County. City-only services are labeled
  Salt Lake City; WoodSpring is explicitly in Bluffdale, with cost and travel
  limits. Online rental search counts under the phone/online rule.
- **Consolidation:** individual authority properties were not split out. UCA rent
  help and mediation have different intake numbers and purposes. CDCU's city
  renter center and its homebuyer service have different phones and eligibility.
  Salt Lake City repairs are not a second listing of the CDCU renter center.
- **Availability:** no vacancy, grant or admission was promised. Housing Connect's
  page advertises openings, but a dynamic portal prints conflicting template text;
  that does not establish the actual current list status. The record therefore
  directs the client to confirm a list rather than declaring it open or closed.
- **Stale sources:** UCA's old `/housing/` URL is replaced by its current program
  page. The Tenant Resource Center's current city page supplies hours, superseding
  the older guide. Salt Lake City's closed Fix the Bricks program is not advertised
  as available. CDCU's past class dates are not future appointments.
- **Source limitations:** LifeStart's current official text was readable through
  the public search index while a page open returned no parsed body. The record
  preserves that evidence limitation and directs admissions questions to the
  provider; it does not inherit old detailed deposit/child-age rules from the
  baseline. Ruff Haven's pages disagree on duration, so no fixed length is promised.
- **Scope discipline:** NeighborWorks and Utah Housing Corporation were not
  declared bad agencies. They were not selected for this bounded trial for the
  reasons in the research ledger. Aspen and the old Road Home shared-housing
  program did not establish a current general fixed-income route. Their omission
  is not a closure claim.

## Types and groups

Nine Types are used: eight original checklist kinds and one proposed addition,
tenant support/eviction prevention. Each narrows the 16 records to 1–3 records;
every record has a supported assignment. The unfilled shared-housing kind stays
in the checklist gap ledger, not as an empty browse filter.

The five For groups describe targeted eligibility, not everyone who might use a
service. Authority and repair programs have specifically evidenced older-adult or
disability pathways; Milestone, LifeStart and SSVF establish the other three groups.
Every record has an explicit yes/no decision for every proposed group in
`decisions.json`. General housing availability did not justify adding veterans,
disability, seniors or single-parent labels to every record. Group intersections
do not promise that a single subprogram accepts every intersection.

## Selection judgment

The 10 starters cover 8/9 original kinds plus the proposed tenant-support kind.
HomeInn and LifeStart are the only deliberately repeated principal kind: single
rooms for adults and apartments for single-parent households are materially
different client options. Tenant Resource Center also has a narrow relocation-aid
Type but is selected for navigation; it is not a second generic rent-aid starter.

The six remaining records have individual consideration reasons. The first five
complements are Milestone, HASLC, Habitat repair, UCA mediation and city repairs;
WoodSpring follows with its substantial commercial-cost/travel limitation. These
add distinct eligibility, property portfolios or assistance terms. No claim that
they cover a previously uncovered original kind is made. Ordering is an evaluation
suggestion only; WSRS-TSO's importer and curation order were not changed.

**Remaining judgment:** Michael's across-the-desk verdict on the 16-item sample is
the success measure. Eight kinds covered is not proof that referrals succeed or
that this method outperforms production on cost.
