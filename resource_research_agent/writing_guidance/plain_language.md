# Writing resources for counseling and printed handouts

Write respectfully for adults seeking practical help. A service missionary must
be able to scan the resource quickly. The person taking the printout home may
need support with reading, remembering steps, or using a computer. Use everyday
words, short sentences, and brief paragraphs or simple bullets. Explain an
unavoidable acronym. Avoid agency jargon, promotional language, and research
process commentary. Do not judge the person's ability or circumstances.

Description: briefly say what help is offered and where it is available. Identify
location and service area accurately; an office address does not establish an
eligibility boundary. Preserve meaningful geographic limits and uncertainty.

Programs and Services: explain specific help, combining overlapping accounts.
Prefer useful service distinctions over a long organizational history or list
of incidental benefits. Do not repeat the same explanation in another section.

Eligibility Requirements: state the relevant qualifications and required
referrals or documents. Preserve material age, income, location, and other
conditions. Requirements may vary by program; never substitute an invented
rule for missing information.

How to Best Connect: give a supported starting route and practical next steps.
Include appointment or walk-in requirements and useful application instructions.
When known, explain what happens after applying without promising placement,
funding, eligibility, or response times. Keep phone, address, website, and hours
in their ordinary fields; avoid duplicating those fields in Information. There
is no dedicated email field: include a useful email address in this section.
Do not assume a computer or internet access. Include an offline alternative only
when supported. Do not invent documents, contacts, language help, or a preferred
intake route.

Access: describe the area served and relevant access arrangements. Distinguish
service area from the physical office or individual job/service locations.

Important Information to Know: give material costs, limitations, availability,
and specific questions the person should check. Distinguish applying from
receiving help. Keep unknown details brief and relevant; do not add generic
warnings to every resource or repeat them under several headings.

Return section bodies only; Scout supplies the exact headings and their order.
Use plain paragraphs and optional lines starting with '- ' for bullets. Do not
return HTML, Markdown links, tables, or extra headings. Use full URLs when a
link is needed on paper. Keep necessary conditions even when shortening text;
there is no mandatory word count. Never append original Scout Findings to the
public Information. Original research stays in the assignment history.

Every claim must be supported by the provided evidence or recorded additional
research. Return writingEvidence with candidateIds (contributing candidate IDs
as strings) and sources (an array). Each newly consulted source must contain
exactly url (full HTTP/HTTPS URL), accessedOn (YYYY-MM-DD), and excerpt (relevant
source text). Use an empty sources array if only the supplied evidence was used.
Evidence storage is not human verification: leave verifiedOn null. If a material
detail cannot be established, say what is unknown or what to ask. Do not copy
known providers, contacts, or eligibility facts from a writing example.
